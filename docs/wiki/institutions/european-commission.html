European Commission

Modified

September 23, 2026

Role in the Rating Process

The European Commission provides bright-line inputs to the Backstop Eligibility parameter (R6) through its debt-sustainability analysis and risk classification. It supplies the gross financing need cross-check for Refinancing Need (R1) via its Debt Sustainability Monitor. The institution runs fiscal surveillance, including assessments under the excessive deficit procedure, which informs Fiscal Capacity (C3-T) and Backstop Eligibility (R6). It publishes the AMECO database, which serves as a source for unit-labour-cost and real-exchange-rate data relevant to Growth (C1-A2) and Inflation Differential (C2-M*).

Evidence by Country

Euro Area

  • The European Commission assesses countries under the EU fiscal framework, monitoring compliance with net expenditure growth rates and the implementation of national medium-term fiscal-structural plans.
  • The institution evaluates the activation of the “national escape clause” for defense spending across several member states, permitting deviations from fiscal rules provided fiscal sustainability is not endangered in the medium term.
  • The European Commission published the Debt Sustainability Monitor 2025, classifying medium-term fiscal sustainability risks for 12 Member States as high, 10 as medium, and 5 as low, based on a no-fiscal-policy-change baseline.
  • The European Commission assessed the fiscal sustainability condition for activating the national escape clause for 16 Member States.
  • The European Commission forecasts that EU GDP growth will slow to 1.1% in 2026 and the Euro area to 0.9%, due to an energy price shock from the Middle East conflict. Growth is projected to recover to 1.4% (EU) and 1.2% (Euro area) in 2027.
  • The aggregate EU general government deficit is projected to widen from 3.1% of GDP in 2025 to 3.6% in 2027.
  • The European Commission notes that fiscal space is narrower than in 2022, with many Member States burdened by legacy costs and rising defense expenditure.
  • The European Commission issued the Spring 2026 European Economic Forecast and provided a toolbox to address the energy crisis, emphasizing targeted and temporary fiscal measures.

Austria

  • The European Commission projects a structural primary deficit of 1.6% of GDP as of 2026, assuming no fiscal policy change from 2027. This level is low compared with past fiscal performance. Ageing-related expenditure is projected to increase.
  • The S2 debt-stabilisation indicator points to medium risk, requiring a 4.2 percentage point improvement in the structural primary balance by 2027 to stabilize debt. This is driven by the initial budgetary position (2.3 pps) and ageing costs (1.8 pps).
  • The S1 debt-reduction indicator points to medium risk, requiring a 3.7 percentage point effort to reduce debt to 60% of GDP by 2070.
  • Gross financing needs are expected to remain large, reaching around 21% of GDP in 2036.
  • A large share of debt is held by non-residents. Contingent liability risks stem from the private sector and state guarantees.
  • The 10-year sovereign yield spread vs. German Bund is 28 basis points as of 31 December 2025.
