European Central Bank

Modified

September 23, 2026

Role in the Rating Process

The European Central Bank publishes the euro-area AAA yield curve that determines the Safe Rate (C4). It provides inflation projections used for the Inflation Differential (C2). The bank reports government bond yields and spreads, which feed into the Haircut calculation (R4). Together with the European Stability Mechanism, the European Central Bank acts as a backstop provider (R6). Holdings of government bonds by the Eurosystem are a key factor in determining the market-held portion of the debt, affecting the Refinancing Need assessment (R1-A*).

Evidence by Country

Euro Area

  • The European Central Bank provides the broader macroeconomic context and euro-area inflation averages against which country-specific inflation is compared.
  • The European Central Bank provides the euro-area inflation average used for comparison.
  • The European Central Bank’s composite indicator of systemic sovereign stress (SovCISS) is used to monitor short-term sovereign risk. The European Central Bank’s policy rates and asset purchase programs are noted in the context of financing conditions and debt holder composition.
  • The European Central Bank participates in the Debt Sustainability Analysis working group. Its composite indicator of systemic sovereign stress (SovCISS) is referenced in market risk charts.
  • The European Central Bank is expected to cautiously tighten its monetary policy stance, with markets pricing in policy rates of around 2.6% in the first quarter of 2027.

Austria

  • The European Central Bank provides data on banking sector resilience, capital ratios, and credit growth.
  • The European Central Bank’s monetary policy normalization has led banks to shift portfolios away from central bank reserves towards government bonds, increasing exposure to sovereign risk.

Belgium

  • The European Central Bank’s data is used to track bank funding structures and non-performing loans, providing context for the banking sector.

Bulgaria

  • The European Central Bank is relevant regarding banking sector data and the context of Bulgaria’s transition to the euro area.
  • The European Central Bank is mentioned in the context of the European Semester and economic governance framework, though no specific actions or data are detailed in the recommendation document.
  • The European Central Bank provided positive assessments for Bulgaria’s euro adoption.
  • Four Bulgarian banks are classified as significant institutions and have been subject to direct supervision by the European Central Bank since 2020.

Croatia

  • The European Central Bank is mentioned indirectly in the context of the European Semester and economic governance framework.
  • The European Central Bank is referenced in the context of climate risk stress tests for commercial banks, which complement domestic supervisory expectations.

Cyprus

  • The European Central Bank publishes data on pension fund assets and banking sector indicators.

Estonia

  • The European Central Bank’s data is used to track bank funding structures and non-performing loans, providing context for the banking sector.

Finland

  • The European Central Bank cut policy rates since June 2024, which has supported private consumption and investment in Finland by lowering borrowing costs.

France

  • The European Central Bank’s Bank Lending Survey reported that French banks slightly tightened credit standards for non-financial corporation loans in the fourth quarter of 2025.
  • The European Central Bank provides data on credit growth and pension fund assets.

Germany

  • The European Central Bank started to ease monetary policy in 2024, reducing policy rates from 4% in June 2024 to 2.25% in April 2025. Market inflation expectations over the next 12 months are well-anchored at the European Central Bank’s target of 2%.

Greece

  • The European Central Bank provided input for the 2025 EU-wide stress test conducted by the European Banking Authority, which confirmed the resilience of Greek banks.
  • Greece’s credit rating returned to investment grade in late 2023, making Greek sovereign bonds eligible for the European Central Bank’s monetary policy operations in the standard collateral framework. This upgrade reflects sustained structural reforms and enhanced banking-sector resilience.

Ireland

  • The European Commission references European Central Bank data on financial sector indicators, such as non-performing loan ratios and capital adequacy.
  • The European Commission notes the European Central Bank’s role in monitoring financial stability.
  • Staff from the European Central Bank participated in post-programme surveillance missions in liaison with the European Commission.

Italy

Latvia

Lithuania

Luxembourg
