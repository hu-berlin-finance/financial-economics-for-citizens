Inflation Differential

Modified

September 23, 2026

In the MRP

Δπ is the projected five-year average HICP (Harmonised Index of Consumer Prices) inflation of the country minus that of the euro area, using projections from the International Monetary Fund World Economic Outlook or the European Central Bank. Common euro-area inflation cancels out of the model, so only the differential matters. The MRP uses min{0, Δπ}: a negative differential is debited in full (about 6.3 percentage points of GDP of borrowing capacity per 100 basis points at the reference calibration); a positive differential is not credited, because differentials in a currency union tend to reverse through competitiveness.

In the DAP

C2-E covers projections that are unavailable or implausible, with a fallback Δπ = 0. C2-T covers stale projections, with red flags such as legislated wage rounds, administered-price resets, or tax changes with mechanical price effects. C2-A covers imperfect indexation, with red flags such as bracket creep or a high share of expenditure indexed by law; these effects go to fiscal capacity, not Δπ. C2-M covers the labour-cost versus consumer-price differential, with a red flag being a unit-labour-cost differential persistently above the HICP differential, which triggers the C1-A2 check. Not crediting a positive differential is a conservative design choice that the DAP does not assess.
