Refinancing Need

Modified

September 23, 2026

In the MRP

The parameter \(n_t\) measures the gross financing need as a share of gross domestic product. It is calculated as the sum of long-term debt divided by its weighted average maturity, short-term debt, and the headline deficit. The data comes from the International Monetary Fund World Economic Outlook, Eurostat, and national debt management offices. Analysts cross-check this value against the gross financing need published in International Monetary Fund Article IV reports or the European Commission’s Debt Sustainability Monitor. This variable acts as the model’s state variable. It is compared with the edges of the fragile band to classify the country as safe, fragile, or distressed.

In the DAP

The discretionary adjustments process for refinancing need covers task R1*. It addresses three issues. R1-E checks for missing or distorted components. Red flags include unavailable maturity or bill data, with the OECD Global Debt Report serving as a fallback. It also flags debt growing faster than deficits explain by more than one percent of gross domestic product per year, and contingent liabilities migrating onto the sovereign. R1-T checks for shifts in financing behavior. Red flags include a rising share of bills or a lower maturity target, and changes in or depletion of the treasury cash buffer. The anchored corner is \(n_t\) net of the cash buffer. R1-A checks for debt not exposed to the market. Red flags include official loans, program financing, and captive holdings. The anchored corner is the market-held refinancing need. A parameter move changes values under the standard definition, never the definition itself.
