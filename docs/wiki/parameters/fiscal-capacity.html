Fiscal Capacity

Modified

September 23, 2026

In the MRP

The parameter \(s̄\) represents fiscal capacity. It is the highest five-year average primary balance as a share of gross domestic product in the country’s own history. Data comes from the International Monetary Fund World Economic Outlook and Fiscal Monitor. This figure shows the surplus the country has demonstrated it can sustain. The parameter \(s_t\) is the current-year projected primary balance. Soft information, such as institutional quality or restructuring history, may place \(s̄\) below this historical estimate. It never places it above the demonstrated maximum. Sensitivity grids typically show \(s̄\) to be the most consequential input.

In the DAP

The discretionary adjustments process assesses fiscal capacity under task C3*. Teams look for three types of issues. C3-E applies when the estimator fails or rests on one episode. Red flags include never having a five-year surplus or relying on one exceptional pre-crisis window. Teams use realism benchmarks or the euro-area median as fallbacks. C3-T applies when history lags policy. Red flags include adopted consolidation or expansion plans and binding corrective paths under the EU fiscal framework. C3-A applies when default is chosen, not just suffered. Red flags include mainstream platforms normalizing restructuring, a collapse of the consolidation consensus, or sustained government instability at high debt.

Debt Sustainability Monitor

  • The document uses a no-fiscal-policy-change baseline from 2027 onwards, anchoring the structural primary balance to the 2026 forecast. This implies that any adjustment committed in medium-term plans beyond 2026 is not included in the baseline projection.
  • The baseline assumes the structural primary balance remains constant at its 2026 forecast level until 2036, except for ageing costs. Alternative scenarios include a convergence to historical average SPB and a lower SPB scenario.
  • The long-term fiscal sustainability indicators S1 and S2 measure the required fiscal effort. S2 is the immediate and permanent one-off change in the structural primary balance needed to stabilize public debt over an infinite horizon. S1 is the change needed to bring debt to 60% of GDP by 2070. Risk categories are assigned based on the size of these adjustments (low if <2% of GDP, medium if 2-6%, high if >6%).
  • The document employs stochastic projections with 10,000 shocks to assess uncertainty, covering 80% of simulated debt paths. Deterministic stress tests include scenarios for adverse interest-growth differentials, lower SPB, historical SPB, and financial stress.
