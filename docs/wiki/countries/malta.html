Malta

Modified

September 23, 2026

Overview

This dossier draws on the 2025 Debt Sustainability Monitor and the Spring 2026 Economic Forecast, both issued by the European Commission in early and mid-2026, alongside the 2026 Country Report issued on 2026-06-03. The evidence characterizes the Malta economy as robust, with real gross domestic product growth projected at 3.7% for 2026, driven by tourism and domestic consumption. Fiscal indicators show a general government deficit stabilizing around 2.2% of GDP and public debt remaining below 47% of GDP, with a projected decline to 40.8% by 2036 under a no-policy-change baseline. Key themes include persistent labor shortages, pressure on housing and infrastructure, and institutional challenges related to the justice system and transparency. Long-term fiscal sustainability assessments indicate high risk for debt stabilization due to ageing costs, requiring significant structural primary balance improvements.

  • C3-A, default is chosen, not only suffered: The tax system faces challenges regarding aggressive tax planning risks, with features creating opportunities for double non-taxation and profit shifting. Malta does not publish tax expenditure reporting.

  • C3*, context: The structural primary balance is projected at -1.0% of GDP on average for 2026-2035. The fiscal consolidation space is 50% in the baseline scenario.
  • C3*, context: The European Commission projects a structural primary deficit of 1% of GDP as of 2026. This is in line with past fiscal performance. Ageing-related expenditure is projected to decrease slightly over the medium term but increase over the long term.

Market Signals

  • Factor decoupling: Business confidence and investment indicators show mixed results, with high administrative burdens and regulatory barriers cited by firms.
  • Rollover pessimism: The 10-year sovereign yield spread vs. German Bund is 91 basis points as of 31 December 2025.

Outlook Material

  • Outlook Factors, rule of law: Concerns persist over the efficiency of the justice system. The time to resolve civil and commercial cases increased from 432 to 491 days between 2016 and 2024. Resources for the judiciary are limited, with a low number of judges per capita.
  • Outlook Factors, rule of law: Transparency issues exist in public procurement and lobbying. Ministers stopped publicly declaring family income after 2023. No comprehensive rules on lobbying or robust asset declaration requirements are in place.
  • Housing and Infrastructure: Rapid population growth (34% increase between 2015 and 2025) has put strain on infrastructure, land use, and public services. Housing costs are a significant driver of poverty, with 7.7% of those at risk of poverty having mortgage or rent arrears in 2025.
  • Energy Security and Climate: Malta is highly vulnerable to climate risks. The energy mix is skewed towards imported fossil fuels. Renewable energy share in electricity mix was 16.7% in 2025, below the EU average of 45%. Fossil fuel subsidies accounted for 1% of GDP in 2024.
  • Labor Market and Skills: Severe skills shortages exist, particularly in STEM (Science, Technology, Engineering, and Mathematics) and green transition sectors. Participation in STEM post-secondary education is significantly below the EU average (13.8% vs 25.2%).
  • Social Protection: Social transfers reduced the monetary poverty rate by only 24.9% in 2025, below the EU average of 33.2%. Vulnerable groups, including third-country nationals and persons with disabilities, face higher risks of poverty and exclusion.
