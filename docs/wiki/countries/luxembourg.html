Luxembourg

Modified

September 23, 2026

Overview

This dossier synthesizes information from the OECD Economic Survey (2025-04-28), the European Commission Debt Sustainability Monitor (2026-02-12), the European Commission Economic Forecast (2026-05-21), and the European Commission Country Report (2026-06-03). The evidence highlights a slowdown in economic growth driven by structural productivity stagnation and external shocks, alongside a shift from fiscal surplus to deficit. Structural challenges include housing affordability, pension sustainability risks due to demographic aging, and low long-term growth potential. The European Commission classifies medium-term debt sustainability risks as low to medium, though long-term fiscal gaps are significant.

Economic

C1* Growth (Growth)

C1-E Broken or dominated sample

No evidence in the sources yet.

C1-T Structural break in trend

  • C1-T, structural break in trend: Real GDP growth slowed to 1.1% in 2020–2025, significantly below the historical mean of 4.5% in 2000–2007 and 2.5% in 2010–2019. Medium-term forecasts project growth of 1.6% in 2026 and 2.0% in 2027, remaining below historical trends.
  • C1-T, structural break: Real GDP growth is expected to accelerate to 1.6% in 2026 and 2.0% in 2027, supported by services exports.

C1-A1 Growth not thin-tailed or serially independent

No evidence in the sources yet.

C1-A2 Inherited competitiveness gap

  • C1-A2, inherited competitiveness gap: Labor productivity has stagnated since the early 2010s, with labor productivity decreasing by 1.6% and total factor productivity falling by 1.8% on average per year between 2021 and 2025. The financial sector, accounting for around 25% of value added, shows negative productivity growth, pointing to diminishing efficiency gains.
  • [C1-A2, competitiveness gap]: Unit labor costs increased by about 15% since the end of 2021, leading to an appreciation in the unit labor cost-based real effective exchange rate, although this has partially reversed as nominal wages in neighboring countries caught up .
  • [C1*, context]: Measured labor productivity in Luxembourg is among the highest in the OECD, but growth has stagnated since 2010, driven by declining multi-factor productivity and slowing capital deepening .

Other growth context

  • [C1*, context]: The OECD projects real GDP growth to recover gradually, reaching 2.1% in 2025 and 2.3% in 2026, supported by easing financial conditions and a gradual recovery in the construction sector .

Fiscal

C3* Fiscal capacity and plan (Fiscal Capacity)

C3-E Estimator fails or rests on one episode

No evidence in the sources yet.

C3-T History lags policy

  • C3-T, history lags policy: Luxembourg was found not compliant with its medium-term fiscal-structural plan commitments in 2025. The annual deviation from the recommended net expenditure path was 1.3% of GDP, and the cumulative deviation was 0.6% of GDP. The general government balance turned to a deficit of 2.0% of GDP in 2025, after a surplus of 0.9% in 2024, driven by higher spending on defense, housing, and climate measures.
  • [C3-T, history lags policy]: Under unchanged tax and spending policies, gross public debt would remain stable until the late 2040s but would rise rapidly thereafter due to aging-related expenditure, particularly pensions .
  • C3-T, history lags policy: The general government balance is forecast to remain in deficit, improving to -1.2% in 2026 and widening slightly to -1.5% in 2027.
  • [C3-T, context]: The OECD recommends adopting a national net spending rule to anchor fiscal policy, replacing the obsolete EU-mandated medium-term objective .

C3-A Default is chosen, not only suffered

No evidence in the sources yet.

Other fiscal context

  • C3*, context: Public debt is projected to increase from 26.5% of GDP in 2025 to 30.2% in 2027, mostly due to budget deficits and stock-flow adjustments. Risks to the fiscal plan include lower than projected economic growth and increasing spending pressures from aging and defense commitments.
  • C3*, context: The European Commission projects a structural primary surplus of 1.4% of GDP as of 2026. This is low compared with past fiscal performance. Ageing-related expenditure is projected to increase.
  • C3*, context: The S2 indicator points to high risk, requiring a 7.1 percentage point improvement, driven by ageing costs (7.8 pps). The S1 indicator points to medium risk, requiring a 2.0 percentage point effort.
  • [C3*, context]: Luxembourg has a history of prudent budget management, with gross public debt below 30% of GDP in 2023 and net government financial assets of about 50% of GDP .
  • [C3*, context]: The fiscal stance is broadly neutral in 2025, but the OECD advises phasing out energy policy supports and accelerating consolidation from 2026 to rebuild fiscal buffers .

National institutions

  • [R6-A, context]: The OECD suggests enhancing the mandate of the national fiscal council to include long-term debt sustainability assessments and to monitor compliance with the new national fiscal rule .

Market Signals

  • Rollover pessimism: The 10-year sovereign yield spread vs. German Bund is 29 basis points as of 31 December 2025.

Outlook Material

  • Housing and Labor Market: High house prices and limited supply pose systemic risks. House prices in 2025 were 60% higher than a decade ago. The unemployment rate rose above the EU average for the first time since 2009, reaching 6.5% in 2025. Skills mismatches are significant, with macroeconomic skills mismatches rising to 34.4% in 2025, far above the EU average of 19.2%.
  • Pension Sustainability: The pension system is projected to start running a deficit as of 2029 due to unfavorable demographic dynamics. The pay-as-you-go Pillar I reserve is projected to be entirely depleted by 2048. A 2025 pension reform increased contribution rates by 1.5 percentage points to 25.5% but is deemed insufficient to fully address structural sustainability issues or significantly increase the low participation rate of workers aged 55–64 (51.9% in 2025).
  • Tax Planning: Aggressive tax planning remains unaddressed. Luxembourg introduced a non-deductibility rule for interest and royalty payments, but its scope is limited. The country is identified as a conduit for capital flows, with net inward FDI stock 31 times larger than GDP.
  • [Pension Sustainability]: The OECD identifies the pension system’s sustainability as a major long-term risk, projecting pension expenditure to rise from 9.4% of GDP in 2024 to 17.5% in 2070 without reform .
  • [Climate Transition]: The transition to green growth will have a net negative impact on the budget balance, estimated at around 2% of GDP by 2050, primarily due to lower fuel revenues .
  • [Housing]: Housing affordability remains low, with real house prices falling by about 20% since late 2022, but affordability deteriorating further due to higher interest rates .
