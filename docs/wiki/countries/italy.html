Italy

Modified

September 23, 2026

Overview

This dossier draws on the OECD’s Economic Survey for Italy, published on 2026-04-23, the European Commission’s 2026 Country Report for Italy, published on 2026-06-03, the European Commission’s 2026 Spring 2026 Economic Forecast, published on 2026-05-21, and the European Commission’s 2025 Debt Sustainability Monitor, published on 2026-02-12. The OECD survey assesses the economy as resilient but facing medium-term headwinds from high public debt, population ageing, and weak productivity growth. The European Commission reports highlight a deceleration in real GDP growth, persistent labor productivity stagnation, and a rise in the debt-to-GDP ratio. The Spring 2026 Economic Forecast projects a slowdown in growth in 2026 due to a global energy shock. The Debt Sustainability Monitor classifies medium-term risks as high, noting large gross financing needs and projected debt increases. Key themes include fiscal sustainability risks under the Medium-Term Fiscal-Structural Plan, regional disparities, demographic challenges, and exposure to global energy price volatility.

Economic

C1* Growth (Growth)

C1-E Broken or dominated sample

No evidence in the sources yet.

C1-T Structural break in trend

  • [C1-T, structural break in trend]: The OECD projects real GDP growth to remain modest at 0.4% in 2026 and 0.6% in 2027, weighed down by a surge in energy prices and geopolitical uncertainty. This follows moderate growth of 0.5% in 2025.
  • [C1-T, structural break in trend]: Real GDP growth decelerated to 0.5% in 2025 due to geopolitical uncertainty and trade barriers. Investment rebounded after a decline in 2024. Exports grew, reversing two years of decline, though imports grew faster.
  • [C1-T, structural break]: Real GDP is expected to grow by 0.5% in 2026 and 0.6% in 2027. Domestic demand is dampened by loss of purchasing power and geopolitical tensions.

C1-A1 Growth not thin-tailed or serially independent

No evidence in the sources yet.

C1-A2 Inherited competitiveness gap

  • [C1-A2, inherited competitiveness gap]: Labour productivity growth has lagged the euro-area average since the mid-1990s. The level of multi-factor productivity declined between 2000 and 2022. The large share of small, low-productivity firms weighs on aggregate performance.
  • [C1-A2, inherited competitiveness gap]: Labor productivity stagnation persists. Italy’s GDP growth has consistently lagged behind employment growth over the past two decades. Unit labor costs rose, contributing to inflation, but the protracted slump in labor productivity weakens competitiveness.
  • [C1-A2, inherited competitiveness gap]: Significant regional gaps persist. Southern regions’ GDP per head is 65% of the EU average, compared to above EU average for the rest of the country. Productivity in the South is ~80% of EU levels versus 99% for the rest of Italy. R&D investment in the South is 1% of GDP versus 1.5% in the rest of the country.

Fiscal

C3* Fiscal capacity and plan (Fiscal Capacity)

C3-E Estimator fails or rests on one episode

No evidence in the sources yet.

C3-T History lags policy

  • [C3-T, history lags policy]: The Italy government has committed to a steady fiscal consolidation path under the Medium-Term Fiscal-Structural Plan (MTFSP) until 2031. This implies primary spending declining relative to GDP each year to reduce the debt ratio.
  • [C3-T, history lags policy]: Primary surpluses are expected to remain insufficient to offset the debt-increasing effects of the interest-growth differential and stock-flow adjustments. As a result, the debt-to-GDP ratio is projected to increase from 137.1% in 2025 to 139.2% in 2027.
  • [C3-T, history lags policy]: The government deficit is projected to fall to 2.9% of GDP in both 2026 and 2027.

C3-A Default is chosen, not only suffered

  • [C3-A, default is chosen, not only suffered]: Public debt exceeds 137% of GDP (Maastricht) and is projected to rise further under current tax and spending settings. Ageing costs are projected to rise by at least 1% of GDP by the mid-2030s, primarily due to pensions.
  • [C3-A, default is chosen, not only suffered]: The European Commission notes high fiscal sustainability risks in the medium term. Sizeable debt servicing costs and skewed public expenditure towards pensions limit budgetary flexibility. The automatic link between retirement conditions and life expectancy is set to resume but will be phased in gradually.

Other fiscal context

  • [C3*, context]: The general government deficit fell to 3.1% of GDP in 2025, supported by stronger revenue from higher employment and wages. It is projected to decline to 2.9% in 2026 and 2027.
  • [C3*, context]: The structural primary balance is projected at 1.1% of GDP on average for 2026-2035. The fiscal consolidation space is 43% in the baseline scenario.
  • [C3*, context]: The European Commission projects a structural primary surplus of 1.1% of GDP as of 2026. This is broadly in line with past fiscal performance. Ageing-related expenditure is projected to increase.
  • [C3*, context]: The S2 indicator points to low risk, as no improvement is needed to stabilize debt due to projected decreases in ageing costs. The S1 indicator points to low risk, requiring a 1.6% of GDP effort.

Debt

R1* Refinancing need (Refinancing Need)

R1-E Components missing or distorted

  • [R1-E, components missing or distorted]: Gross refinancing needs are currently near 25% of GDP. The term structure of outstanding debt is near the euro-area average at close to 8 years.
  • [R1-E, components missing or distorted]: Stock-flow adjustments related to the delayed cash effect of housing renovation tax credits contribute to the rise in debt.
  • [R1-E, components missing]: The debt-to-GDP ratio is set to rise to 139.2% by 2027, driven by a debt-increasing interest-growth-rate differential.
  • [R1*, context]: Gross financing needs were 23.3% of GDP in 2025. Debt is projected to increase to 149.1% of GDP by 2036.
  • [R1*, context]: Gross financing needs are expected to remain large, reaching around 27% of GDP in 2036.

R1-T Financing behavior shifts

No evidence in the sources yet.

R1-A Part of the debt not exposed to the market

  • [R1-A, part of the debt not exposed to the market]: Insurers and pension funds hold significant amounts of government debt. Italian insurers hold roughly 10% of the outstanding stock of Italian government bonds. Pension fund assets were EUR 243 billion at end-2024.
  • [R1-A, context]: Risk-increasing factors relate to the share of short-term government debt. Risk-mitigating factors include the major share of debt held by domestic lenders and euro denomination. The favourable net international investment position mitigates fiscal risks.

Other refinancing context

  • [R1*, context]: The debt-to-GDP ratio reversed its downward trajectory in 2024 and started rising again, driven mainly by the lagged impact of tax credits for housing renovations.

C5* Haircut (Haircut)

C5-A Politically feasible haircut differs from 30%

  • [C5-A, politically feasible haircut differs from 30%]: Domestic banks hold a relatively large share of government bonds (approx. 25% of bank assets). The average residual duration of these holdings has risen to near 4.8 years, exposing banks to deterioration in the government bond market.

Market Signals

  • [Factor decoupling]: Interest spreads have narrowed and agencies have upgraded credit ratings. The spread with the 10-year German government bond yield has decreased significantly in recent years.
  • [Factor decoupling]: The report notes that global trade uncertainty poses a major challenge for export-driven economies like Italy.
  • [Rollover pessimism]: The 10-year sovereign yield spread vs. German Bund is 74 basis points as of 31 December 2025.

Outlook Material

  • [Energy security]: The 2026 Middle East conflict has renewed volatility in global energy markets. Italy remains exposed due to high reliance on imported fossil fuels (74% of net total energy consumed).
  • [Trade policy]: Global trade policy restrictions and competition from emerging economies (e.g., China) weigh on Italian exports and industrial production.
  • [Demographic Challenges]: Italy faces long-standing demographic challenges. By 2050, the total population is expected to decline by more than 4 million. The fertility rate was 1.18 in 2024, well below the EU average of 1.34. The share of the population under 15 decreased to 11.9% in 2025.
  • [Brain Drain]: A significant outflow of young, highly qualified Italians seeking opportunities abroad persists. In 2024, around 188,000 people left the country, while 450,000 migrated to Italy.
  • [Labor Market Segmentation]: Atypical work remains common. 11.3% of workers had involuntary temporary contracts and 8.5% were in involuntary part-time roles. Undeclared work concerned about 3.13 million full-time equivalent jobs in 2023.
