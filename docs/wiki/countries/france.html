France

Modified

September 23, 2026

Overview

This dossier draws on the European Commission’s 2025 Debt Sustainability Monitor, published on 2026-02-12; the European Commission’s Spring 2026 Economic Forecast, published on 2026-05-21; the European Commission’s 2026 Country Report, published on 2026-06-03; and the OECD’s 2026 Economic Survey, published on 2026-06-30. The evidence highlights persistent economic stagnation, with real GDP growth projected between 0.7% and 0.8% for 2026 and 2027. Fiscal challenges are significant, as the general government deficit remains above 5% of GDP and debt is projected to rise above 144% of GDP by 2036 under a no-policy-change baseline. Structural issues include weak labor productivity, high public spending, slow progress in renewable energy deployment, and political uncertainty stemming from recent government changes and a hung parliament.

Economic

C1* Growth (Growth)

C1-E Broken or dominated sample

No evidence in the sources yet.

C1-T Structural break in trend

  • C1-T, structural break in trend: The European Commission projects real GDP growth to remain subdued at 0.8% in both 2025 and 2026, driven by energy price increases, fiscal consolidation, and the fallout from the conflict in the Middle East. Growth was partly offset in 2025 by less restrictive monetary policy and strengthening domestic demand, but private consumption remained sluggish due to high household saving rates (18.2% of gross disposable income in 2025) .
  • C1-T, structural break: Economic activity is forecast to grow at 0.8% in 2026 and 1.1% in 2027. Net exports supported by aeronautics and defense offset weak private consumption due to higher energy prices.

C1-A1 Growth not thin-tailed or serially independent

No evidence in the sources yet.

C1-A2 Inherited competitiveness gap

  • C1-A2, inherited competitiveness gap: France’s price competitiveness has improved, with favorable performance in unit labor cost and GDP deflator-based real depreciations compared to other EU Member States in 2024 and 2025. However, world export market shares declined in 2024 due to weak demand in main markets and underperformance in key sectors like automotive and aeronautics .

Other growth context

  • C1*, context: Labor productivity growth remains lacklustre. Although labor productivity (112.7% of the EU level in 2025) picked up in 2024 and 2025, it is constrained by low capital deepening and stagnant total factor productivity. Structural challenges include low business R&D intensity (2.18% of GDP in 2024) and limited adoption of advanced digital technologies by Small and Medium-sized Enterprises .
  • C1*, context: Real GDP growth slowed to 0.9% in 2025 amid heightened political uncertainty and is projected to slow further to 0.7% in 2026 before picking up slightly to 0.8% in 2027.
  • C1*, context: GDP per capita growth has lagged behind highest-income OECD economies, growing by only 63% of the average European Union country rate between 2000 and 2025.
  • C1*, context: Political uncertainty since 2024, marked by multiple government changes and a hung parliament, has complicated consensus-building and reduced GDP growth by an estimated 0.2–0.4 percentage points.
  • C1*, context: The employment rate reached a historical high of 69.6% in the second quarter of 2025, but the unemployment rate is projected to increase to 8.3% in 2026.
  • C1*, context: Labour productivity has recovered to pre-pandemic levels but remains limited by weak technology adoption and skills shortages.
  • C1*, context: Real GDP growth is projected at 0.2% in 2025, 1.2% in 2026, and averaging 0.7% from 2028 to 2036.

C3* Fiscal capacity and plan (Fiscal Capacity)

C3-E Estimator fails or rests on one episode

No evidence in the sources yet.

C3-T History lags policy

  • C3-T, history lags policy: France committed to placing debt on a downward trajectory under its medium-term fiscal-structural plan for 2025–2029, aiming to reduce the deficit below 3% of GDP by 2029. However, the general government deficit is projected to remain at 5.1% of GDP in 2026. Political gridlock in 2026 budget adoption weakened deficit reduction plans, and the suspension of the 2023 pension reform until January 2028 is expected to worsen the budget balance and increase public debt in the medium term .
  • C3-E, history lags policy: The S2 indicator points to medium risk, requiring a 3.1% of GDP improvement in 2027 to stabilize debt. The S1 indicator also points to medium risk, requiring a 3.9% of GDP effort to reduce debt to 60% by 2070.
  • C3-T, history lags policy: The general government deficit is forecast to remain at 5.1% in 2026 and rise to 5.7% in 2027. Large primary deficits and rising interest payments drive debt increases.

Other fiscal context

  • C3*, context: Public expenditure remains high at 57.2% of GDP in 2025, the second highest in the Eurozone. Approximately 60% is earmarked for social protection and healthcare. The European Commission notes that spending reviews have identified around EUR 10 billion in savings for 2025–2026, but a more ambitious strategy is needed to permanently reduce spending and improve efficiency .
  • C3*, context: The tax burden is significantly higher than the EU average, with tax revenues at 44.3% of GDP in 2025. High taxes on production (4.4% of GDP) and labor (implicit tax rate of 38.8% in 2024) weigh on growth. Fiscal and social tax expenditures amount to nearly 6% of GDP, complicating the tax system and posing a heavy budgetary burden .
  • C3*, context: Public spending reached 57.2% of GDP in 2025, which is 7.4 percentage points above the euro area average.
  • C3*, context: Stabilizing public debt at 122% of GDP by 2030 requires substantial fiscal consolidation, with a cumulative adjustment of around 3 percentage points of GDP compared to the current primary budget balance.
  • C3*, context: The fiscal deficit was 5.1% of GDP in 2024 and is projected to decline to 4.6% by 2027, provided consolidation continues.
  • C3*, context: Ageing-related spending pressures are significant, with pension benefits high and the old-age dependency ratio rising from 38% in 2022 to 53% in 2050.
  • C3*, context: Tax expenditures represent 2.9% of GDP, contributing to a complex tax system with limited systematic evaluation.
  • C3*, context: The structural primary balance is projected at -2.1% of GDP on average for 2026-2035. The fiscal consolidation space is 81% in the baseline scenario.
  • C3*, context: The SPB before cost of ageing is assumed at -2.1% of GDP from 2026 onwards. This is low compared with past fiscal performance.
  • C3*, context: The European Commission projects a structural primary deficit before cost of ageing of -3.0% of GDP in 2026, stabilizing at -2.1% from 2027 onwards.
  • C3*, context: The S2 indicator requires a 3.1% of GDP effort to stabilize debt, driven by the initial budgetary position (3.0%) and ageing costs (0.1%). The S1 indicator requires a 3.9% of GDP effort.

Bank–sovereign nexus and captive creditors

Willingness to pay and political stability

  • Political uncertainty: Political gridlock and uncertainty are expected to persist until presidential elections in mid-2027, complicating the meeting of medium-term fiscal commitments. The suspension of the 2023 pension reform adds to fiscal uncertainty .
  • Political Stability: Heightened political uncertainty and frequent government changes since 2024 have weakened business and household confidence, delaying consumption and investment.

Market Signals

  • Factor decoupling, rollover pessimism: The document does not provide specific data on government bond spreads or yields. However, it notes that higher borrowing costs are contributing to the rise in interest payments as a share of GDP .

Outlook Material

  • Regional disparities: Territorial disparities are increasing, with traditional heavy industry regions in the North and North-East facing economic vulnerabilities and high youth outmigration. Outermost regions face persistent structural vulnerabilities, with GDP per capita significantly below the EU average (e.g., 30% in Mayotte) .
  • Energy transition: Challenges persist in deploying renewable energy, with slow permitting and insufficient grid investments. The share of renewable energy sources in gross final energy consumption reached 23.2% in 2024, but France is not on track to meet the EU 2030 target .
  • Geopolitical Risks: The evolving conflict in the Middle East has triggered an energy shock that disrupted economic momentum and weighed on investor sentiment.
  • Industrial Competitiveness: Manufacturing deindustrialization has been sharper than the OECD average, driven by structural shifts and weaker export performance.
  • Contingent Liabilities: Contingent liability risks from the private sector, including state guarantees from the COVID-19 crisis, are risk-increasing factors.
  • Climate Risk: Under the “Fragmented Action” climate scenario, debt could be around 6 pps. of GDP higher in 2050.
