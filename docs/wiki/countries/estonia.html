Estonia

Modified

September 23, 2026

Overview

This dossier draws on the European Commission’s Debt Sustainability Monitor published on 2026-02-12, the European Commission’s Spring-2026-Economic-Forecast published on 2026-05-21, and the European Commission’s Country Report and Country-Specific Recommendations published on 2026-06-03, alongside the OECD’s Economic Survey published on 2026-06-08. The documents assess Estonia’s economic and fiscal trajectory amid geopolitical headwinds, specifically Russia’s war in Ukraine and a new global energy shock from the Middle East. Key themes include the activation of the national escape clause under the Stability and Growth Pact to accommodate increased defense spending, projected to reach 5.0% of GDP in 2026. Consequently, the general government deficit is projected to widen to 4.5% of GDP in 2026 and 4.8% in 2027. The European Commission classifies Estonia’s medium-term debt sustainability risks as medium, with gross debt projected to rise to 56.2% of GDP by 2036 in the baseline scenario. The recommendations emphasize sustaining defense spending while broadening the tax base, particularly via property taxes, and addressing structural issues such as regional disparities, access to finance for small and medium-sized enterprises, and energy security. The OECD adds that competitiveness losses and demographic headwinds persist, and highlights challenges in climate mitigation and uneven artificial intelligence adoption.

C1-A2 Inherited competitiveness gap

  • C1-A2, inherited competitiveness gap: Unit labour costs in manufacturing rose significantly more than in neighbouring Baltic economies, leading to a loss of cost competitiveness and a 15% fall in export market share volume between 2022 and 2024.
  • C1-A2, inherited competitiveness gap: Large wage increases following the energy shock eroded competitiveness. Credit growth slowed initially but revived in late 2025 as interest rates fell. Consumer sentiment remains downbeat due to high uncertainty.

  • C1*, context: The document highlights persistent regional disparities, with economic activity concentrated in the capital region, while remote and rural regions face demographic decline and structural challenges.

  • C1*, context: Business research and development spending as a share of GDP remains below the European Union average, and patenting has declined.

Fiscal

C3* Fiscal capacity and plan (Fiscal Capacity)

C3-E Estimator fails or rests on one episode

  • C3-E, history lags policy: The S2 indicator points to medium risk, requiring a 2.9% of GDP improvement in 2027 to stabilize debt. The S1 indicator also points to medium risk, requiring a 2.4% of GDP effort to prevent debt from exceeding 60% by 2070.

C3-T History lags policy

  • C3-T, history lags policy: Fiscal policy turned countercyclical in 2026. The budget deficit is projected to widen to 4.3% of GDP in 2026 and 4.7% in 2027, driven by increased defence spending (to 5% of GDP) and tax cuts (basic tax allowance increase).
  • C3-T, history lags policy: The OECD recommends implementing a gradual medium-term fiscal consolidation from 2027 to stabilise debt.
  • C3-T, history lags policy: The general government deficit is projected to widen significantly from 2.0% of GDP in 2025 to 4.5% in 2026. This is driven by increased defense spending (activated national escape clause), rising welfare and healthcare costs, and revenue losses from a transition to a universal tax system.
  • C3-T, history lags policy: The Council of the EU endorsed Estonia’s national medium-term fiscal-structural plan covering 2025–2028, with maximum growth rates of net expenditure of 7.1% in 2025, 5.1% in 2026, 3.6% in 2027, and 3.2% in 2028.
  • C3-T, history lags policy: The Council of the EU activated the national escape clause for Estonia in 2025, allowing deviation from recommended maximum growth rates to support increased defence spending until 2028.
  • C3-T, history lags policy: The general government deficit is set to widen to 4.5% of GDP in 2026 and 4.8% in 2027.

C3-A Default is chosen, not only suffered

  • C3-A, default is chosen, not only suffered: Estonia is applying the escape clause of the EU fiscal rules for defence spending until 2028.

Other fiscal context

  • C3*, context: Public debt is low but rising, from 24.1% of GDP in 2025 to 26.9% in 2026. The tax base is narrow, heavily reliant on labor and consumption taxes (90% of total tax revenue in 2024). Recent tax increases include VAT (22% to 24%) and personal/corporate income tax (20% to 22% in 2025).
  • C3*, context: Defense spending increased from 2% of GDP in 2021 to 4.2% in 2025, expected to reach 5.0% in 2026. The Council activated the national escape clause to allow this increase.
  • C3*, context: Net expenditure grew by 2.0% in 2025, which was below the recommended maximum growth rate. Cumulatively for 2024–2025, growth was 5.7%, also below the recommended rate.
  • C3*, context: Net expenditure is projected to grow by 12.0% in 2026, exceeding the recommended maximum growth rate by a deviation of 2.8% of GDP in annual terms. The cumulative growth for 2024–2026 is projected at 18.4%, a deviation of 1.5% of GDP. This deviation is within the flexibility of the national escape clause.
  • C3*, context: The general government deficit increased from 1.1% of GDP in 2024 to 2.0% in 2025. It is projected to reach 4.5% of GDP in 2026 and 4.8% in 2027. The 2026 deficit increase reflects decreased revenue from personal income tax reform and increased defence spending and infrastructure investment.
  • C3*, context: The fiscal stance was contractionary by 0.5% of GDP in 2025, projected to be expansionary by 2.8% of GDP in 2026, and contractionary by 0.3% of GDP in 2027.
  • C3*, context: General government tax revenue was 35.2% of GDP in 2024, four percentage points below the European Union average. Measures include increasing VAT to 24%, raising personal and corporate income tax rates to 22%, and introducing a motor vehicle tax.
  • C3*, context: Property taxation revenues amounted to just 0.3% of GDP in 2024, well below the European Union average of 1.8%. The Commission recommends broadening the tax base, including via taxes on vacant residential property.
  • C3*, context: Total general government defence expenditure amounted to 4.2% of GDP in 2025 and is projected at 5.0% of GDP in 2026.
  • C3*, context: Social benefit expenditure remained below the European Union average at 17.8% of GDP in 2024, compared to 27.3% for the European Union.
  • C3*, context: The structural primary balance is projected at -3.1% of GDP on average for 2026-2035. The fiscal consolidation space is 89% in the baseline scenario.
  • C3*, context: The SPB before cost of ageing is assumed at -3.1% of GDP from 2026 onwards. This is low compared with past fiscal performance.

Outlook Material

  • Climate transition: Estonia remains among the most carbon-intensive OECD economies. Emissions reductions in transport and agriculture have lagged, and land use/forestry has shifted from a carbon sink to a source.
  • Climate adaptation: Climate risks, particularly flooding, are moderate but require early investment and stronger governance. Municipalities lack financial and administrative capacity for adaptation.
  • AI adoption: AI adoption is high in knowledge-intensive sectors but lags in traditional sectors. Skill shortages are a key barrier to wider adoption.
  • Outlook Factors, geopolitical shocks: Geopolitical uncertainty from Russia’s war in Ukraine and the war in the Middle East weighs on growth, exports, and energy prices. Estonia faces risks to critical grid infrastructure from hybrid attacks.
  • Outlook Factors, banking events: The banking sector is resilient with a CET1 ratio of 19.7% (Q3 2025), well above the EU average. Credit growth accelerated in 2025, reaching 12% for NFCs and 9.9% for households annualized.
  • Outlook Factors, structural reforms: Reforms are ongoing to improve access to finance, particularly for SMEs in remote regions. The investment account regime was expanded in 2025 to include crowdfunding and crypto assets.
  • R6*, context: The document does not explicitly discuss Backstop Eligibility parameters like ESM loans or ECB program financing in a negative context. However, it notes that Estonia successfully synchronized its electricity grid with the EU in February 2025, a key sovereignty and stability marker.
  • Outlook Factors, energy security: Estonia remains vulnerable to severe electricity price spikes. While solar power penetration is high (67.9% of renewables in net electricity generation in 2025), the construction of more battery energy storage systems is essential.
  • Outlook Factors, structural reforms: The implementation of cohesion policy programmes is above the average pace at the European Union level. The document emphasizes sustaining reforms under the Recovery and Resilience Facility.
  • Outlook Factors, social challenges: Early school leaving was above 10% in 2025, and youth unemployment was above 20% in 2025. Poverty risks are high for persons with disabilities (32.5% of working-age population with disabilities) and older women living alone.
  • Investor Base: The large share of government debt held by non-residents is a risk-increasing factor.
